Stop-loss vs Trailing Stop vs Break-even

November 21, 2026 · 4 min read

After you open a position, the real work begins: limiting the loss and protecting the profit. The three most common tools for this are stop-loss, trailing stop and break-even. They all have "stop" in their name, but they do different jobs.

Stop-loss: limiting the loss

A stop-loss is an order that closes the position when the price reaches a level you set. Its purpose is to keep the loss on a trade at a predefined level. The level is set when the position opens and stays where it is unless you change it.

One thing to note: during sudden price moves a stop order may fill not at your exact price but at a worse one, with slippage. A stop-loss limits the loss but does not guarantee the exact price.

Take-profit: exiting at the target

A take-profit closes the position when the price reaches your target level. Used together with a stop-loss, both ends of the trade are defined in advance.

Trailing stop: a stop that follows the price

A trailing stop moves the stop level at a set distance as the price moves in your favour. When the price turns back, the stop stays where it is and the position closes at that level. This way you stay in the position while the trend continues and try to keep part of the profit when it reverses.

  • If the distance is too tight, normal fluctuations can take you out early.
  • If the distance is too wide, you can give back most of the profit on a reversal.
  • The right distance depends on the coin's volatility and the timeframe you trade.

Break-even: moving the stop to entry

Break-even means moving the stop level to the entry price after the price reaches a certain profit. If the trade then reverses, you aim to exit at least at your entry price. Using a small offset that places the stop slightly beyond entry to cover commission is a common approach.

Break-even has a cost too: if the stop is moved to entry too early, a normal pullback can take you out of the trade without profit.

How do you set the stop level?

There is no single right way to set a stop level; three common approaches are:

  • Percentage-based: placed a fixed percentage below (or above) entry. Simple, but ignores the coin's volatility.
  • Volatility-based: set according to a volatility measure such as ATR. Gives a wider stop on volatile coins and a tighter one on calm coins.
  • Chart structure: placed slightly beyond technical levels such as the latest low or high.

Whichever method you choose, think about stop distance and position size together. With a wider stop you can carry the same risk with a smaller position.

Common mistakes

  • Opening a trade without a stop-loss, or saying "I'll add it later".
  • Moving the stop further away as the price approaches it; this can turn a limited loss into an unlimited one.
  • Setting the trailing stop distance tighter than the coin's normal fluctuation.
  • Applying break-even too early and exiting without profit on every small pullback.
  • Placing the stop exactly at entry without accounting for commission.

How to use them together

These tools are not alternatives; they complement each other. A common flow: the trade opens with a stop-loss and take-profit; when the price reaches a certain profit, the stop is moved to entry; if the trend continues, a trailing stop follows the profit. Closing part of the position at the target and carrying the rest with a trailing stop is also a common method.
