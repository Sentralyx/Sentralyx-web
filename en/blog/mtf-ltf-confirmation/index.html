What Is MTF and LTF Confirmation?

November 21, 2026 · 3 min read

The same coin can be rising on the 15-minute chart while falling on the 4-hour chart. Which chart should you trust? Multi-timeframe confirmation evaluates a signal against several periods instead of just one.

Why does the timeframe matter?

Each timeframe shows a different side of the market. Short timeframes produce more signals but are more exposed to noise; long timeframes give fewer signals that reflect the bigger picture. Multi-timeframe confirmation tries to combine these two views.

MTF: higher timeframe confirmation

With MTF confirmation, a signal on your trading timeframe is compared with the direction of a larger timeframe. For example, when a BUY signal forms on the 15-minute chart, it is accepted if the trend on the 4-hour chart is also up; otherwise it is filtered out.

  • Benefit: reduces trades opened against the main trend.
  • Cost: produces fewer signals and may notice trend reversals late.

LTF: lower timeframe confirmation

With LTF confirmation, the signal is confirmed on a smaller timeframe. For example, when a signal forms on the 4-hour chart, the trade is entered once a move in the same direction has started on the 15-minute chart. The goal is to improve entry timing.

  • Benefit: helps catch a better entry moment on a signal in the right direction.
  • Cost: some opportunities are missed if the lower timeframe does not confirm.

Using them together

Used together, MTF and LTF create a three-layer structure: the higher timeframe sets the direction, the trading timeframe produces the signal and the lower timeframe times the entry. Each layer reduces the number of signals, so it is healthier to add filters one by one and measure their effect.

How to choose timeframes

A common approach is to keep a ratio of about 3 to 6 times between timeframes, for example 15 minutes and 1 hour, or 1 hour and 4 hours. Timeframes that are too close give almost the same information; timeframes that are too far apart can look unrelated. You can find the combination that fits your strategy by comparing them with backtests.

Example scenario

Say your strategy runs on the 1-hour chart. On one coin, RSI and MACD produce a BUY signal together. With MTF confirmation enabled, the app checks whether the trend on the 4-hour chart is up. If the 4-hour chart is in a downtrend, the signal is filtered out, which means avoiding a trade against the main trend.

If the 4-hour trend is also up and LTF confirmation is enabled, the app then checks whether an upward move has started on the 15-minute chart. If it has, the trade opens; if not, it waits. The result is fewer but more aligned signals. This alignment does not guarantee profit; it only selects situations where different timeframes point the same way.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing timeframes that are too close (e.g. 5 and 10 minutes) and treating them as separate confirmations.
  • Enabling every filter at once and being surprised that almost no signals remain.
  • Not re-running the backtest after adding a filter.
  • Assuming a filter works by looking at results from a single period.

Together with other filters

Timeframe confirmation is not the only filter. ADX, which measures trend strength, ATR, which measures volatility, and market regime filters (trend, sideways, volatile) also help weed out signals. Too many filters, however, can lead to a strategy that produces almost no signals.
